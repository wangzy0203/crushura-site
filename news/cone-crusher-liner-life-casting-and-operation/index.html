Cone Crusher Liner Life: Half Casting, Half Operation

Home / News / Cone Crusher Liner Life: Half Casting, Half Operation

Liner life is decided half in the foundry and half in operation. Full-chamber feeding, feed distribution, the closed side setting and complete-set replacement all decide how long they last.

2026-10-09

Cone crusher liner life is decided in two places. Half of it is set in the foundry: the material the liner is cast from and the process used to make it. The other half is decided on the job — how the crusher is fed, how the chamber is set, and how the machine itself is maintained. A liner that is cast well can still be worn out early by the way the crusher runs.

Run the Chamber Full

Running the chamber full is the first operating rule. When the feed is light, the chamber is not filled, and the load is not spread across the liner surface. It concentrates on part of the liner instead. Two things then go wrong at once: output falls, and wear speeds up, because the same work is being done on a smaller area of liner. Filling the chamber spreads that load over the whole liner.

Feed Distribution

Where the material lands decides which part of the liner wears first. If the feed goes to one side of the chamber, that side is worn through while the rest of the liner still has life left in it. The adjustment is at the feed plate: move the drop point so the material is distributed around the chamber instead of being dropped in one place. This is a setting the plant can check and correct.

Closed Side Setting

A closed side setting that is kept too small over a long period accelerates wear. It also increases the risk of tramp iron. Both effects build up slowly, which is why this setting belongs on a measuring schedule rather than being looked at only when something seems wrong.

Replace the Set, Not Half of It

Liners are not a one-part decision. When the time comes, the fixed cone, the moving cone and the eccentric bushing should be assessed together, as a set. Replacing only half of that set leaves the crushing chamber profile mismatched, and the mismatch shows up in the results: output drops and particle shape gets worse.

The Hydraulic System

The hydraulic system carries the same consequences as the wear parts, and should be judged the same way: the hydraulic station, the hydraulic cylinders, the seals and the piping. Tramp iron protection depends on all of them working. If that protection fails once, the repair can cost as much as a complete set of liners.

Material and Process

On the manufacturing side, two items are named as key to extending the overhaul interval of the whole machine: surface hardening of the main shaft, and quenched and tempered eccentric bushings in 20CrMnTi or 42CrMo. These are not the parts that wear against the rock, but they help set how long the machine can run between major overhauls.

The Measuring Routine

Two measurements at two intervals cover most of the operating picture:

  • Closed side setting — measure once a week.
  • Liner thickness — measure once a month.

Those two numbers are the operating half of liner life.
