Belt Conveyors: The Parts That Fail First

Home / News / Belt Conveyors: The Parts That Fail First

Idlers, head pulley lagging, belt tracking, bucket elevator chains and magnetic separators are where a conveyor line fails first. Here are the checks and the spares to keep.

2026-10-09

On a conveyor line, the part that fails first is never the belt.

That is the pattern on belt conveyors and bucket elevators alike. The failures start with the parts around the belt: the idlers under it, the lagging on the head pulley, the chain and sprockets in a bucket elevator, and the magnetic separator that protects the crusher downstream. This article covers those wear points and how to plan spares for them.

Idlers: a Rotation Check, Done by Batch

A belt conveyor runs on upper idlers and lower idlers. When one of them seizes, it stops turning, and the belt no longer rolls across it. The belt rubs against a stationary roller instead, and that grinds a ring mark into the belt. A ring mark is not cosmetic: it is where the belt tears next.

So the check is a rotation check. Go around by batch and confirm that each idler still turns freely. A seized idler does not stop the belt, so it has to be found by checking, not by watching output.

When the check finds a seized idler, the fix is a part change — provided the part is already on the shelf. That is what the batch check is for.

Head Pulley Lagging: Polished Smooth, Then Slipping

The wear point on a pulley is the lagging on the head pulley. Once the lagging has been polished smooth, the pulley slips. Output drops, and the belt is damaged at the same time.

The criterion is the lagging surface. Lagging that still has its pattern grips. Lagging that has been polished smooth is where the slipping starts, and by then the belt is being damaged too.

Belt Mistracking: Three Common Causes

Belt mistracking has three common causes. Check them before anything else:

  • Idlers not square — the idler brackets are not square to the belt line.
  • Feed off-centre — material is not landing centrally on the belt.
  • Take-up unequal on the two sides — tension is not the same on the left and on the right.

The three are worth checking in that order, because each one is visible on the machine: an idler that is not square, a feed that lands to one side, a take-up that pulls harder on one side than the other.

Bucket Elevators: Chain, Sprocket, Bucket

On a bucket elevator, the wear parts are the chain, the sprockets and the buckets. They are not independent. A slack chain starts to jump teeth, and once it does, it takes the sprocket with it — chain and sprocket are scrapped together. That is the failure to prevent, and the check that prevents it is chain tension.

Chain is a stock item. Catching a slack chain early is what keeps the sprocket out of the scrap bin.

Magnetic Separators: Selection Follows the Material Layer

The magnetic separator is the insurance for the crusher downstream of it, and it belongs in the same spares plan as the parts above. It has one selection rule: magnetic force is chosen according to the thickness of the material layer.

Spare Parts Planning

The spares list for belt conveyors and bucket elevators comes down to two rules.

  • Idlers, brackets, chains and the magnetic separator: keep one set.
  • Belt: keep it by the meter.

Idlers, brackets, chains and the magnetic separator are kept as one set, so that a check finding can be fixed from the shelf. Belt is kept by the meter rather than as a set.

The pattern is the same in every case. The small part fails first, and if it is not caught it takes the larger part with it. A seized idler tears the belt. A slack chain scraps the sprocket. Polished head pulley lagging costs output and damages the belt. Checking idlers by batch, watching the lagging, checking chain tension and working through the three tracking causes are what keep a failure at part level instead of belt level.
